AgriPrice-NG Phase 3.4 final validation

Selection protocol: each transformation (original, log1p, price_change, percentage_change) was fitted on the chronological training period and selected using validation RMSE only. The selected configuration was refit on training plus validation rows, then evaluated once on the untouched test months.

Audit finding: the Phase 3.3 transformation report ranked candidates by the fixed-period test RMSE (its rows contain `rmse` and `naive_rmse`, and the accompanying diagnosis names the lowest test-RMSE method). Those rankings are retained as historical diagnostics, but they were not reused for this final selection.

Interpretation of the earlier error gap: the tree models learn from small, non-stationary training windows and are predominantly interpolators, so they shrink forecasts toward historical levels when the later test regime moves. The naive rule carries the latest calendar-month level directly and therefore adapts immediately to level shifts. Movement targets reduce this mismatch in some series, but they do not guarantee an improvement.

The naive baseline is the previous observed calendar-month value in the feature table. No target prices were interpolated or forward-filled. Movement-target predictions were reconstructed from the lag available before each target month.

Series evaluated: 6; successful non-naive metric rows: 24.
XGBoost: available (3.4.1).
TensorFlow: unavailable: No module named 'tensorflow'.

Test dates are comparable within each series. LSTM uses complete 12-month observed sequences; its sequence test dates can differ from the tabular feature test dates because the sequence window requires 12 preceding calendar months.
Recursive forecasts use predicted prices as future history for all subsequent steps; they do not access actual future prices and therefore accumulate uncertainty, especially where historical coverage is irregular. Forecast recursion starts from the observed monthly series and only uses calendar-contiguous recent history for the lag/sequence window.

Descriptive untouched-test outcome (not a selection rule): validation-selected XGBoost has lower RMSE than the naive benchmark for Oil (palm), while validation-selected stacking is lower for Rice (imported) 50 KG; validation-selected Gradient Boosting is lower for Yam. The naive benchmark remains lower for Millet, Rice (imported) 2.8 KG, and Rice (local). These results are series-specific and should not be pooled across units.
