Recommended training plan based on this dataset
------------------------------------------------
1. Target and series definition
   Use price as the single target, in nominal NGN and in its observed unit.
   Keep currency == NGN (the only value present). Do not combine KG, bags,
   litres, pieces, tubs, or other units into one numeric series. A modeling
   series should be identified by at least:

       commodity + market_id + unit + pricetype

   priceflag should be kept as a series-selection/status field. The primary
   experiment should use one consistent flag per series (prefer actual where
   there is enough history); aggregate and actual,aggregate observations must
   not be silently mixed with actual observations. Record the final policy and
   selected series in the training configuration.

2. Monthly preparation
   The source is already monthly, so do not resample daily or weekly data.
   Parse date as a true datetime and use the observed month (the source date is
   always the 15th). Sort chronologically and aggregate only if a duplicate
   monthly key is encountered after the documented filtering. Reindexing a
   selected series to a monthly calendar may be used to expose gaps, but do
   not fill a missing target with a fabricated price. Drop rows whose lag or
   rolling features cannot be computed from past observations.

3. History sufficiency and sparsity
   There are 1,753 commodity-market-unit-price-type series. Before splitting,
   742 of these have at least 36 observed records, 562 have at least 48, and
   289 have at least 60. These are counts before splitting by priceflag.
   Individual series are not dense: no series meets both 36 observations and
   80% coverage of its full calendar span. Therefore use a configurable
   minimum-history rule (for example 36 observed monthly points plus a
   documented maximum-gap rule) and exclude a series that cannot support a
   12-month lookback and the required validation/test horizon. Save the exact
   selected combinations rather than claiming that all markets are modeled.

4. Features without leakage
   For each selected series, create lagged prices (for example lags 1, 2, 3,
   6, and 12 observed/calendar months as supported), shifted rolling means and
   standard deviations (3, 6, and 12), month number, and cyclic month
   features. Every rolling statistic must be shifted so it uses only data
   available before the prediction date. Fit any scaler or encoder on the
   training portion only. Do not use usdprice as an input derived from the
   target unless this relationship is explicitly treated as leakage; the
   safer default is to exclude it.

5. Chronological evaluation
   Use the same cutoff dates and the same selected series for all five models.
   A practical split is an earlier training period, a chronological validation
   period, and the final chronological test period; use rolling-origin
   validation when a series has enough observations. Do not randomly shuffle.
   For a six-month evaluation, require six future observed target months after
   the cutoff; otherwise record that the series cannot support that horizon.
   Include a persistence/last-observed-price naive baseline.

6. Required models and comparable target
   Train the LSTM (TensorFlow/Keras), Random Forest Regressor, XGBoost
   Regressor, Gradient Boosting Regressor, and a stacking ensemble on the same
   price target and comparable chronological test period. The tree models can
   use the lag/rolling/calendar feature table. The LSTM should use the same
   past-price information as a fixed-length chronological sequence. For the
   stacking model, generate out-of-fold chronological predictions from the
   base models, fit the meta-model only on those predictions, and evaluate it
   once on the untouched test period.

7. Metrics and forecasts
   Report RMSE, MAE, MAPE, and R-squared for every model and series. Compute
   MAPE only where the actual price is non-zero; keep the zero-count in the
   report. Produce actual-versus-predicted plots and CSV comparison tables.
   One-, three-, and six-month forecasts must be generated recursively: after
   predicting one month, append that prediction to the lag/rolling history
   before predicting the next month. A one-step result must not be relabeled as
   a six-month forecast. If confidence intervals are added, document whether
   they come from residual bootstrap or another method and state that they are
   approximate for sparse, non-stationary market series.

8. Outputs and reproducibility
   Preserve data/raw/. Write cleaned monthly data to data/processed/, models
   and preprocessing objects to models/, plots to outputs/plots/, and metrics
   and configuration to reports/ and CSV outputs. The configuration should
   include the target column, unit/priceflag policy, selected series, date
   cutoffs, lookback, forecast horizons, random seeds, and package versions.
   Only after this inspection and approval of the plan should the full training
   pipeline and dashboard be implemented.

Conclusion
----------
The supplied WFP file is a complete, monthly, 54,883-row Parquet table with
42 commodities, 68 observed markets in 14 states, NGN prices, multiple price
types and 20 recorded units. It has no missing values or duplicate observation
keys, but the commodity-market series are uneven and sparse. The main modeling
decision is therefore series selection and unit/priceflag consistency; a
single model trained on all raw price values would mix incomparable units and
would not be a defensible price forecast.
